# ADR-005: Dashboard Display Stats vs. Authoritative Financial Metrics

**Status**: Accepted (revised)
**Date**: January 2024
**Decision Makers**: Founder / Product Owner

---

## Context

The merchant and driver dashboards need to display aggregate figures. Two categories of figure were originally treated as one:

1. **Operational display stats** — total orders, average rating, total deliveries (count-based, non-financial)
2. **Financial metrics** — delivery fees charged, platform commission retained, driver earnings

These have different accuracy and auditability requirements. A single ambiguous `total_revenue_egp` column conflated both categories and created a risk of using a stale display cache in a financial context.

---

## Decision

**Split into two explicit strategies, one per category.**

### Strategy A — Denormalised display columns (non-financial only)

Store pre-calculated, non-financial display counts in the `merchants` and `drivers` tables. Recalculate daily (or on admin demand).

Columns in scope:
- `merchants.total_orders`
- `merchants.total_delivery_fees_egp` — total delivery fees billed (display only)
- `merchants.total_platform_commission_egp` — WASL commission retained (display only)
- `merchants.average_rating`
- `drivers.total_deliveries`
- `drivers.total_earnings_egp` — driver payout total (display only)
- `drivers.average_rating`
- `drivers.cancellation_rate`

**Rule**: These columns are for dashboard display only. They must never be used to produce invoices, process settlements, or resolve disputes.

### Strategy B — Live PostgreSQL queries (financial truth)

For any financial purpose — invoicing, settlement processing, dispute resolution, reconciliation — query `financial_transactions` and `orders` directly. See `DATABASE_DESIGN.md` for the canonical query for each metric.

The financial transaction record stores the actual EGP amount at the moment of delivery. This amount is correct even if commission rates change in the future.

---

## Why Two Strategies

### Why Keep Denormalised Columns At All

Dashboard loads are frequent (merchants and drivers check stats throughout the day). Without denormalised counts, every dashboard load requires an aggregation query over potentially thousands of rows. At MVP scale this is tolerable, but the denormalised approach costs almost nothing and keeps dashboards fast from day one.

Non-financial counts (`total_orders`, `average_rating`) have no auditability requirement. 24-hour staleness is acceptable for these.

### Why Not Use Denormalised Columns for Financial Figures

- Denormalised columns are **stale** (up to 24 hours). An invoice or settlement must reflect real-time data.
- Denormalised columns are **derived from a recalculation process** that could fail silently. A failed recalculation produces incorrect financial figures.
- Denormalised columns introduce **ambiguity**: does `total_delivery_fees_egp` include cancelled orders? Only delivered ones? What period? A direct query makes these parameters explicit.
- The `financial_transactions` table is **immutable** — records are never updated, only inserted. This is the correct data source for any financial audit.

The original `total_revenue_egp` column was ambiguous about whether it represented total delivery fees or WASL's platform commission. Separating them into `total_delivery_fees_egp` and `total_platform_commission_egp` removes the ambiguity for display purposes while making clear that authoritative figures come from live queries.

## Financial Metric Definitions

### `total_delivery_fees_egp` (display column in merchants)

**Definition**: Sum of `delivery_fee_egp` across all orders with `status = 'delivered'` belonging to this merchant.

**Represents**: Total delivery fees billed to the merchant by WASL (what the merchant paid in total for delivery services).

**Live query**: See `DATABASE_DESIGN.md` — "Merchant: Total Delivery Fees Paid".

---

### `total_platform_commission_egp` (display column in merchants)

**Definition**: Sum of `amount_egp` from `financial_transactions` where `transaction_type = 'order_commission'` and `related_merchant_id` matches.

**Represents**: The portion of the delivery fee that WASL retained as platform revenue.

**Important**: The per-order commission amount is stored in `financial_transactions` at the time of delivery. It is not recalculated from a rate. This means historical records remain accurate even if the commission rate changes in the future.

**The commission rate itself is an unverified assumption** — see [BUSINESS_MODEL.md](../../BUSINESS_MODEL.md).

**Live query**: See `DATABASE_DESIGN.md` — "Merchant: Platform Commission Retained by WASL".

---

### `total_earnings_egp` (display column in drivers)

**Definition**: Sum of `amount_egp` from `financial_transactions` where `transaction_type = 'driver_payout'` and `related_driver_id` matches.

**Represents**: Total earnings paid (or pending payment) to the driver.

**Live query**: See `DATABASE_DESIGN.md` — "Driver: Total Earnings".

## Recalculation Process (for display columns)

Triggered by admin on demand, or on a daily schedule once background jobs are available.

```sql
-- Recalculate merchant display stats
UPDATE merchants m SET
    total_orders                  = sub.order_count,
    total_delivery_fees_egp       = sub.fees,
    total_platform_commission_egp = sub.commission,
    average_rating                = sub.avg_rating
FROM (
    SELECT
        o.merchant_id,
        COUNT(*)                            AS order_count,
        COALESCE(SUM(o.delivery_fee_egp),0) AS fees,
        COALESCE(SUM(ft.amount_egp),0)      AS commission,
        COALESCE(AVG(o.customer_rating),0)  AS avg_rating
    FROM orders o
    LEFT JOIN financial_transactions ft
        ON  ft.related_order_id    = o.id
        AND ft.transaction_type    = 'order_commission'
    WHERE o.status = 'delivered'
    GROUP BY o.merchant_id
) sub
WHERE m.id = sub.merchant_id;

-- Recalculate driver display stats
UPDATE drivers d SET
    total_deliveries   = sub.delivery_count,
    total_earnings_egp = sub.earnings
FROM (
    SELECT
        related_driver_id,
        COUNT(*)                   AS delivery_count,
        COALESCE(SUM(amount_egp),0) AS earnings
    FROM financial_transactions
    WHERE transaction_type = 'driver_payout'
    GROUP BY related_driver_id
) sub
WHERE d.id = sub.related_driver_id;
```

## Audit & Integrity

1. Before each recalculation, log current column values to `audit_logs`
2. After recalculation, compare old vs. new values
3. Alert if `total_orders` decreases unexpectedly (impossible under normal operation — signals data corruption)
4. Provide an admin endpoint to trigger recalculation on demand for a specific merchant or driver

## Staleness Table

| Column | Staleness | Acceptable Use |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| `total_orders` | Up to 24 hours | Dashboard display ✅ |
| `total_delivery_fees_egp` | Up to 24 hours | Dashboard display ✅ |
| `total_platform_commission_egp` | Up to 24 hours | Dashboard display ✅ |
| `average_rating` | Up to 24 hours | Dashboard display ✅ |
| `total_deliveries` | Up to 24 hours | Dashboard display ✅ |
| `total_earnings_egp` | Up to 24 hours | Dashboard display ✅ |
| Any of the above | Any | Invoice / settlement / dispute ❌ — use live query |

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Dashboard stats load instantly (simple SELECT)
- Financial metrics are unambiguous — separate columns, separate definitions
- Historical commission amounts preserved accurately even if rates change
- Audit trail always points to `financial_transactions` as single source of truth
- No risk of using a stale cached value in a financial decision

**Negative / Trade-offs:**
- Recalculation process must maintain two financial display columns correctly
- Slight added complexity vs. a single `total_revenue_egp` column
- Developer must remember the "display only" rule during implementation

---

## Future Review

Revisit this decision if:
- Stats staleness causes operational complaints (real-time dashboard demanded)
- Recalculation takes longer than 5 seconds (signals scale requiring optimisation)
- Background jobs (Celery) are introduced — trigger recalculation on order delivery event

**Upgrade path**: Replace batch recalculation with event-driven incremental updates (increment `total_orders` on delivery, add to `total_delivery_fees_egp` on delivery). More accurate, less batch overhead.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Single `total_revenue_egp` Column
Rejected. Definition was ambiguous (delivery fees vs. platform commission). Risk of misuse in financial context. Replaced by two explicitly named and documented columns.

### No Denormalised Columns (live queries only)
Considered. At MVP scale, live aggregation on every dashboard load is feasible. However, denormalised columns cost almost nothing and keep dashboards fast from day one. Decided to keep them with a strict "display only" rule.

### PostgreSQL Materialized Views
Rejected for MVP. More complex to set up and refresh. Worth reconsidering if the recalculation batch process becomes a bottleneck.
